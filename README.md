# Microclimatic Variations across Different Spatial Units of Jahangirnagar University

## Overview

This repository documents the research project titled **"Microclimatic Variations across Different Spatial Units of Jahangirnagar University."**

The study investigates spatial and temporal variations in microclimatic conditions across the Jahangirnagar University campus, Savar, Dhaka, Bangladesh. It examines the relationships between air temperature, land surface temperature (LST), land use and land cover (LULC), and vegetation characteristics to understand how different landscape features influence local climatic conditions.

The research integrates climate reanalysis data, satellite-derived environmental indicators, GIS-based spatial analysis, statistical methods, and field-based observations.

## Research Objectives

The study aims to:

•	To measure key micro-climatic parameters (e.g., temperature, humidity, wind speed, solar radiation) across various land use types such as forested areas, water bodies, open fields, and built-up zones within the university campus.

•	To assess spatial and temporal variations in micro-climate conditions using GIS-based mapping and statistical analysis.

•	To identify the environmental and structural factors (e.g., vegetation cover, surface materials, elevation) influencing micro-climate variability across the campus.

## Study Area

**Location:** Jahangirnagar University, Savar, Dhaka, Bangladesh.

The campus represents a heterogeneous landscape comprising forested areas, water bodies, open fields, and built-up areas.

## Key Findings

- Monthly maximum air temperature reached approximately 37°C during May.
- Spatial LST ranged from 24.28°C to 31.99°C.
- Annual maximum LST varied between approximately 28.54°C and 30.05°C during 2011–2025.
- The estimated linear LST trend was −0.041°C per year, although the trend was not statistically significant.
- NDVI analysis identified five categories of vegetation density.
- Four distinct microclimatic zones were identified across the campus.
- Dense vegetation was generally associated with cooler conditions, whereas built-up and exposed surfaces were associated with warmer conditions.

## Research Outputs

## Research Limitations

The study is limited by the spatial resolution of satellite-derived datasets and the availability of environmental observations. The monthly and seasonal temperature analysis does not capture daily microclimatic cycles or short-term extreme events.

Future research may incorporate higher-resolution satellite imagery, additional climatic variables, and more detailed field measurements.
